# EU Icons and Labels

The bundled files are exact copies of the official Commission icon archives,
renamed for stable use. The icon is optional; the applicable Article 50
disclosure is not. An icon alone does not establish legal compliance.

Article 50 itself requires clear, distinguishable, accessible information no
later than first exposure. The more detailed design and placement rules below
come from the voluntary Code and are commitments for its signatories. For a
non-signatory they are an official implementation pathway, not automatically
the only lawful method.

Before choosing an icon, apply the criminal-law exceptions in the decision
tree. Article 50(4) does not require the deployer disclosure when the use of a
deepfake or public-interest text is authorised by law to detect, prevent,
investigate, or prosecute criminal offences. A law-enforcement context alone
does not establish that exception. Record the exact authorisation; other legal
or provider-side duties may remain.

## Choose the semantic variant

| Variant               | Bundled filename prefix | Use under the current Code                                                                                                               |
| --------------------- | ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fully AI-generated    | `ai-generated-`         | Entire in-scope deepfake or published text was generated by AI, with no human-created elements or editorial control apart from prompting |
| Partially AI-modified | `ai-modified-`          | Pre-existing human-made content was partially changed with AI into an in-scope deepfake or public-interest text                          |
| Basic AI              | `ai-basic-`             | Pair with a precise text label or an accessible interactive second layer that explains the AI involvement                                |

Do not use the basic icon as an unexplained generic badge. The Commission's
user testing found better performance when the icon included or accompanied a
text label.

At the dated baseline, the Commission had not published a separate audio-only
EU icon; the Code stated that one would be developed. Do not invent an
official-looking audio mark. Recheck the live icon page and use the audible
disclosure workflow for audio-only content.

## Choose the visual variant

Each semantic icon is included as SVG and PNG in four official variants:

- `black` for a light background;
- `white` for a dark background;
- `black-50` for a light background when the official 50% treatment remains
  sufficiently perceivable in the actual context;
- `white-50` for a dark background when the official 50% treatment remains
  sufficiently perceivable in the actual context.

Prefer SVG on the web. Use the supplied asset without changing letter
proportions. Select a high-contrast variant after testing the real background;
the filename does not prove sufficient contrast.

## Plain-language companion text

Use wording that communicates the artificial origin or modification, not a
vague possibility:

- fully generated: `Generated with AI`;
- partially modified: `Partially modified with AI`;
- basic icon with details: `The voice was generated with AI`,
  `The face was modified with AI`, or another accurate description;
- chatbot: `You are interacting with an AI system.`;
- audio-only opening: `This audio was generated with artificial intelligence.`
  or an accurate manipulated-content variant.

Translate for the audience and keep `AI` only when it is commonly understood.
Do not use `AI-assisted` where the legal fact to disclose is that content was
artificially generated or manipulated.

## Placement and accessibility

The following list follows the current Code and Commission icon page:

- Make the disclosure immediately recognisable without a click, hover,
  scrolling hunt, or sustained attention, at the latest at first exposure.
- Keep it separated from other overlays and visible against every background.
- Embed it in the content unless an equivalent persistent interface overlay is
  available. Aim to preserve it through distribution, download, and reshare.
- Keep a time-limited disclosure visible long enough for people with cognitive
  or processing difficulties.
- When adjacent text already communicates the meaning, mark the icon itself as
  decorative (`alt=""`) to avoid duplicate screen-reader speech. If the icon
  stands alone, give it an accurate accessible name and an accessible way to
  reach any second-layer details.
- Never rely on colour alone. Interactive details must be keyboard operable,
  focus visible, and exposed to assistive technology.

For video, audio, text, and artistic works, follow the modality-specific rules
in [generation workflow](generation-workflow.md).
