# Article 50 Decision Tree

Apply the current live legal text first. Use this tree to organise the facts,
not to override a later amendment or authoritative interpretation.

## 1. Fix the actor

- **Provider:** develops an AI system, has it developed, and places it on the
  EU market or puts it into service under its own name or trademark. A website
  owner can be a provider of its own chatbot or generator.
- **Deployer:** uses an AI system under its authority for professional or
  organisational activity. A company publishing content produced for it is
  normally the deployer even when staff or contractors operate the tool.
- The same organisation can have both roles. Assess each role independently.

## 2. Is there direct AI interaction?

If an AI system is designed for a genuine direct two-way exchange with a
natural person, assess Article 50(1):

- inform the person from the start of the first interaction;
- use a clear and distinguishable, accessible notice;
- apply the `obvious interaction` exception restrictively and document why an
  average reasonably informed, observant, and circumspect person would know.

Suggested English notice: `You are interacting with an AI system.`
Do not hide the first notice only in terms, privacy text, or a help page.

## 3. Does the organisation provide a generative AI system?

For a provider of a system that generates or manipulates synthetic audio,
image, video, or text, assess Article 50(2):

- output must be marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as
  artificially generated or manipulated;
- the solution must meet the current effectiveness, interoperability,
  robustness, and reliability standard as far as technically feasible;
- check the current exceptions for assistive standard editing, no substantial
  alteration, and legally authorised criminal-law uses;
- do not mistake a visible website badge for this provider-side technical duty.

When relying on a third-party model or generator, record what markings and
detection service the provider actually supplies. Reliance does not prove that
your own provider duty is fulfilled.

## 4. Is it a deepfake?

For image, audio, or video, test all current elements:

1. it was AI-generated or AI-manipulated;
2. it has a high level of resemblance to an existing person, object, place,
   entity, or event, including what the current Guidelines treat as plausibly
   existing or having plausibly existed;
3. in the intended and foreseeable context it could falsely appear authentic
   or truthful to the relevant audience.

If all elements are met, a professional deployer must disclose the artificial
generation or manipulation at first exposure under Article 50(4) and (5).
Machine-readable provenance alone is insufficient.

For evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional, or analogous work,
the disclosure remains but may be made appropriately so it does not hamper
display or enjoyment. Do not convert this limited regime into a no-label rule.

## 5. Is it public-interest text?

Test all three content criteria stated in current Commission guidance:

1. the text is published;
2. it is intended to inform the public;
3. it concerns a matter of public interest.

Current examples include politics, public administration and services,
justice, law enforcement, fundamental rights, public security, public health,
environmental protection, consumer safety, and economic, financial,
scientific, political, or cultural developments relevant to public debate.

If all criteria apply, disclose AI generation or manipulation unless both are
true:

- there was substantive human review or editorial control by a person with
  relevant knowledge and professional judgement; and
- a natural or legal person holds ultimate editorial responsibility for the
  publication.

Spelling, grammar, formatting, or purely procedural checks are not substantive
review. Record the responsible role and actual process; do not invent an
individual review log when the current Code does not require one.

## 6. Other Article 50 cases

For emotion recognition or biometric categorisation, assess Article 50(3) and
the privacy regime. Inform exposed persons of the system's operation unless a
current legal exception applies.

## 7. Common website outcomes

| Website use                                    | Likely Article 50 route                           | Required next check                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Customer chatbot                               | Article 50(1) provider notice                     | Is the site the provider; is AI interaction genuinely obvious?         |
| AI portrait of a real or plausibly real person | Article 50(4) deepfake route                      | Resemblance, authenticity, context, audience                           |
| AI product visual                              | Possible deepfake and consumer-law routes         | Existing/plausible object plus risk of misleading product presentation |
| AI article on public policy                    | Article 50(4) text route                          | Purpose, public interest, review, editorial responsibility             |
| Reviewed marketing copy                        | Often outside Article 50(4) text duty             | Other consumer, advertising, IP, or sector rules                       |
| Basic retouching                               | Possible Article 50(2) standard-editing exception | Actual operation and whether input or meaning changed substantially    |
| Private draft never exposed to the public      | Usually no deployer disclosure yet                | Later publication and provider-side marking remain separate            |

`Likely` is not a verdict. Resolve every fact against the live sources and the
actual publication context.
